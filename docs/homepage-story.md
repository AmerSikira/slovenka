# Homepage messaging and structure

Updated Bosnian and English homepages in October 2026. The existing warm canvas, green accents, open columns and restrained editorial typography continue through the new sections.

## StoryBrand materials consulted

- Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand*, publisher-authorized opening chapter excerpt, [Cokesbury PDF](https://cdn.cokesbury.com/samples/pdf/9780718033323.pdf), PDF pages 3–12. The excerpt stresses clear customer needs, the problem the offer solves, and the customer's resulting outcome. This was an excerpt review, not a review of the complete book.
- [Official StoryBrand Online Marketing Course Workbook](https://storybrand.com/downloads/StoryBrand-Online-Marketing-Course-Workbook.pdf), sections on the customer as hero, the brand as guide, a simple plan, calls to action, success and the website audit (printed pages 44–50 and 63–65). Downloaded directly and read through extracted text because the PDF exceeds the browser tool's size limit.
- [Official framework introduction](https://storybrand.com/learn-the-framework/), checked for the emphasis on clear messages and the seven-part approach.

Interpretation and application are specific to this site; no framework text has been reproduced in customer copy.

## What was missing, and what changed

The former hero chiefly described company experience. It now immediately explains the two visitor paths: choose ready-made clothing or discuss manufacturing for a brand. The inquiry is the direct next step; browsing the collection is the lower-commitment alternative.

The own-facility/white-label offer was not explicit. A new section describes both and addresses the practical customer problem of wanting a collection without operating a factory. It explains the benefit of focusing on the brand while agreeing the production scope with MK Slovenka.

The homepage lacked an understandable cooperation plan. A new ordered three-step section names project review, scope agreement, and production/handover. The manufacturing page now uses exactly the same three steps, incorporating samples and approvals into the scope step instead of creating a fourth step.

A closing project invitation clarifies what to prepare: garment description, approximate quantity and preferred timeline. It connects the customer's idea with a clear plan and gives another direct inquiry link. Existing shopping sections and the garment-focused closing link remain accessible.

No customer testimonials, certifications, delivery guarantees, minimum quantities or unverified service promises were added. Supporting services, including labels, are subject to the agreed project scope. Existing inquiry forms still explain that the preview saves locally rather than sending submissions. Lead capture or automated follow-up was not added because the static preview has no backend.
